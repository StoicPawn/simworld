# SimWorld material economy foundation

## Purpose

The geopolitical target requires material processes that exist before states, markets or classes. Economic power must be able to emerge from geography, access, work, property, inventories, obligations and networks rather than from scripted roles.

## Distinct concepts

Never collapse the following into one scalar:

- physical resource truth;
- access to a resource;
- possession;
- ownership/right;
- effective control;
- productive capacity;
- labour;
- inventory/stock;
- consumption;
- exchange;
- wealth;
- debt/obligation;
- information about any of the above.

A person may own land but lack effective control. A household may control an asset without recognized ownership. A rich actor may lack food. A poor actor may control a strategic route. These distinctions are future geopolitical causes.

## Current implementation

### Assets and rights
`Asset` represents material or productive things. `PropertyRight` is temporal, partial and typed. `PropertyRegistry` preserves historical ownership and supports partial transfers.

### Inventories
`Inventory` stores actual quantities of goods owned/held by an economic actor. Material scarcity is therefore distinct from abstract wealth.

### Production
`ProductionProcess` converts labour and material/environmental context into output. Productivity depends on continuous factors such as land quality, climate, tools, security and inputs. No household is assigned a permanent economic caste.

### Exchange
`ExchangeProposal` and `execute_exchange` implement constrained bilateral transfer. Exchange requires actual stock and acceptance. The integrated slice uses geography plus social connection to make encounters/exchanges more or less feasible.

### Integrated vertical slice
Each active household receives productive capacities and a spatial field asset. Annual household material production depends on:

- living labour capacity;
- local fertility;
- local timber availability;
- climate/harvest conditions;
- tools;
- debt/security pressure;
- heterogeneous continuous household skills.

Households consume grain from real inventory. Shortages can increase debt and household stress but do not directly invoke a political outcome.

Households with complementary surplus/shortage may barter if geography permits contact and the exchange is accepted. Rejected exchanges are historical events too.

## Geopolitical direction

Later layers will extend the same primitives into:

- explicit land parcels and territorial mosaics;
- common, private, institutional and contested rights;
- rent, tribute, tax and labour obligations;
- credit and debt networks;
- storage, transport and spoilage;
- roads, ports and market accessibility;
- firms, guilds and other organizations;
- endogenous specialization;
- price formation or alternative allocation institutions;
- concentration of assets across generations;
- land/property inheritance;
- coercive extraction and protection;
- authority emerging from control of resources, routes, labour and information;
- strategic resources whose importance changes with technology and knowledge.

## Invariants

1. Scarcity is a material condition, not a scripted event generator.
2. Need never implies a unique action.
3. Ownership does not imply control; control does not imply legitimacy.
4. Wealth does not imply liquidity, food security or productive capacity.
5. Economic specialization should emerge from heterogeneous circumstances and learning where possible.
6. Geography constrains production and exchange.
7. Actors may be ignorant or mistaken about stocks, rights, resources and values.
8. Rights and transfers are historical facts with temporal provenance.
9. Economic networks must be able to become political networks later without being declared political in advance.
10. Material processes must remain compatible with aggregation and adaptive resolution at planetary scale.
